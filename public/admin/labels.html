          Where the wording comes from

          Your 59 fragrances are already loaded. Each one was taken from FizzyWhiz's own "10% Non Hazardous Base Wax Melt CLP Label" for that oil — their document for melts poured at 10% in a non-hazardous base. Nothing was written by hand. If Michelle pours at a different strength, or in a different wax, these are the wrong documents and they all need redoing from the matching sheets.

          Every fragrance oil supplier publishes a CLP data sheet (sometimes called a CLP calculation or allergen sheet) for each oil. It tells you the exact hazard wording for that oil at the percentage you use it — 6% in wax gives different wording from 10%.

          That sheet is the only valid source. Wording from another maker's label, from another oil, or from a different percentage is not transferable, and the labels are legally your responsibility as the supplier.

          What a GB CLP label has to carry

          • Your business name, full address and telephone number — an email address on its own is not enough
          • The product identifier (the fragrance name)
          • The signal word, Warning or Danger, where one applies
          • Hazard pictograms, where the classification calls for any
          • Hazard statements (the H phrases, written out in words)
          • Precautionary statements (the P phrases)
          • Allergens, as "Contains … May produce an allergic reaction."
          • The net quantity, if it isn't shown elsewhere on the pack

          A batch number isn't required by CLP, but it is worth adding — if a pour ever goes wrong it tells you which one.

          Size

          For packs up to 3 litres the label must be at least 52 × 74 mm, with each pictogram at least 16 × 16 mm. The text has to be easy to read — keep it at 7 pt or above. Smaller labels are only acceptable on very small packs where the full information appears on the outer packaging.

          The minimum is not big enough for your range. Of your 59 fragrances, 38 fit 52 × 74 mm; the other 21 are the ones that can cause an allergic skin reaction, and they carry a signal word, a pictogram, the sensitiser name and six or seven precautionary statements. 76 × 95 mm fits all 59, and 70 × 90 mm fits all but Clean Cotton. The line under the preview counts it for whatever size you pick, so you can check before buying a box of labels.

          The label has to be visible when the customer is buying, so on a sealed bag it goes on the outside, not tucked in with the melts.

          Pictograms

          These are the official United Nations GHS symbols, not redrawn copies, so they are the right shape and the right red. Each one prints at 16 × 16 mm, which is the minimum for a label of this size.

          Most of your melts need no pictogram, and that is correct. The pictogram and the signal word both follow from the hazard statements, and not every hazard statement calls for one. "Harmful to aquatic life with long lasting effects" carries neither; nor does an allergen declaration on its own. Of your 59, twenty-one carry the exclamation mark with Warning, seven carry the environment symbol, and the remaining thirty-six are correctly bare.

          Adding a symbol a fragrance doesn't need is over-labelling. It is not a safe default — it makes the symbols that do matter easier to ignore, and enforcement treats an inaccurate label as a non-compliant one. The checklist under the preview compares what you have ticked against the hazard statements, so it tells you when a symbol is missing and when one is spare. The "Set from the hazard statements" button in the editor fills them in for you.

          Sample packs

          A pack with several different melts in it is several different mixtures in one package, and each one keeps its own label wording. The wording is never merged into one list — that would say every melt in the pack carries hazards that most of them don't, which is as wrong as leaving one off.

          So the pack label is a strip of panels that folds down to the size of one panel and sticks to the back of the clamshell. The front panel carries the pack name, your business details and the net quantity. Then each fragrance gets a numbered block with its own signal word, symbol, statements and allergens. Put a matching number, or the name, on each melt.

          Under Sample packs, tick the fragrances in the pack and set the panel size: measure the space on the back of the clamshell, and that is one panel. The page works out how many panels the pack needs and warns if a strip is too wide for an A4 sheet. Multi-layer peel-back labels are sold for exactly this, or print the strip on sticker paper and concertina-fold it.

          If a pack needs more panels than you'd like, the levers are: fewer fragrances in the pack, picking melts whose labels are short (the ones with no symbol are a third the length of the ones with one), a taller panel, or a smaller text size — but not below 7 pt.

          A4 sticker sheets on the colour inkjet

          The 105 × 74 mm, 8 per sheet size is for plain A4 sticker sheets (sold as "8 labels per sheet, 105 × 74 mm", two across and four down with no gaps) printed on the Canon PIXMA. Every fragrance fits, with the text at 7 or 8 pt, the warning diamonds print red as CLP requires, and the size is over the 52 × 74 mm minimum. Print the first run on plain paper, hold it against a label sheet up to the light, and use the nudge boxes if the printer is a millimetre out. This is the size to use for anything with a warning symbol.

          The Phomemo M110 label printer

          The three roll sizes are the rolls that come with the printer: 50 × 80 mm (the big one, every fragrance fits), 50 × 30 mm and 40 × 30 mm. The printer is thermal, so no ink, and it prints one sticker per label. Print from a computer with the Phomemo driver installed, choosing the printer and the matching paper size in the print box; the phone app cannot print this page directly.

          Two things to know. First, it prints black only, and CLP requires the diamond frame around a warning symbol to be red. So the fragrances with a symbol (the ones that can cause an allergic skin reaction, or are harmful to aquatic life) cannot be properly labelled on this printer: for those, print on a colour printer or buy labels with the red diamond pre-printed. The page tells you which ones. Second, all three rolls are a little under the 52 × 74 mm CLP minimum (the 50 × 80 by 2 mm of width; the small two by a lot), so the small ones are only defensible on small packs where the full details are on the outer packaging too. Anything whose wording does not fit is left off and listed in red rather than printed cut short.

          Not sure?

          The HSE publishes the GB CLP guidance, and most fragrance suppliers will answer questions about their own sheets. If a sheet says something this page has no box for, put it in the "Anything else" field and it prints at the bottom.
